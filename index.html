How this prototype resolves a move
  • Your character card carries a fixed stat line. Your ability is dealt at random from a pool of twelve at the start of the route, so the same climber plays differently each game. Abilities are marked ALWAYS, PER ATTEMPT or PER ROUTE.
  • The tiles come in sets of 15, and every route is a whole number of sets: 15, 30 or 45 holds. So the mix of hold types is identical in every game, one rest ledge per set, and the tank grows with the route.
  • The crag is one shuffled deck, dealt face down at random. No difficulty gradient, no guaranteed rest ledge.
  • What remains hidden is public knowledge, since the deck is fixed. You can count what is left, but never where it is.
  • Once a climber has topped out, everyone else gets one last turn. On that turn only, anyone two or more rows below the summit may lunge, attempting a hold two rows straight up for the row toll plus 1 drop. So from row 3 you still have a shot at the top, and from row 2 you are genuinely out of it.
  • Move up into either hold above you, or sideways along your row. No downclimbing. One climber per hold, except the rest ledge and except a Poacher, who can take an occupied hold and swap places with the climber on it.
  • Rows 1 and 2 are free to reach for. From row 3 up, every attempt costs 1 drop before the dice, win or lose. If that drop alone would empty you, the hold is out of reach and you must rest first.
  • Then roll. Three category dice, each showing 0, 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, plus one condition die rolled with them. A face-down hold turns over after the roll, and one point of shortfall is forgiven for climbing blind.
  • The condition die has six faces: +1, −1, −1, drop, drop, slip. Both act on ONE category, never on your total. The +1 goes where you are furthest short, your choice. The −1 is not your choice: it lands on the category the hold demands MOST, the crux of the move, so you only escape it if you happened to overshoot there. A drop returns 1 water, a slip costs 1.
  • A requirement of 3 needs a rolled 3, or a 2 with a +1. Chalk counts as a flat 2 and ignores your modifiers. A -1 in a category makes a 3 there impossible to match, so you will always pay something on those holds.
  • Each hold type comes in several versions with different numbers, keeping one signature category fixed. A flat edge might be 2/1/1, 1/2/1 or 1/1/2, so the name tells you the family, never the exact demand.
  • The deck demands each category about equally, 40/42/42 in total, and most holds need 2 or more in at least two categories. A single buff cannot clear a hold on its own. A 0 means that category is free on that tile.
  • Character boosts add 1 to a category, after the hold is face up, once per attempt.
  • Missing 3 or more and there is no way back down. You fight for it and pay a drop per point missing, and if you do not have the drops you come off and fall 2 rows. So the moment you reach for a hold you are accepting that risk, whatever your water.
  • Missing 1 or 2 you still have a choice. Take the hold and pay a drop per point, or back off for 1 drop and stay where you are.
  • Matched everything: take it free. Short by any amount: pay 1 drop per point to force the move, or pay 1 drop to back off and stay where you are. The hold stays face up either way.
  • A rest ledge returns 2 drops when you establish it. Chalking up on your turn instead of climbing returns 1, or 2 if nothing above you is free.
  • Drops leave you in exactly four places: the row toll before the dice, a slip on the condition die, forcing a move you came up short on, and backing off. Rows 1 and 2 have no toll at all.
  • Running out of water is the ONLY way to come off the wall. It can happen from the height toll, from forcing a move, or from backing off. Water symbols are counted first, so they can save you.
  • When you empty your water you drop 2 rows, landing on that hold in your column and turning it face up, then refill to full. Below row 3 that means the ground. If the landing hold is taken you drop another row.
  • Play solo against the route with 3 lives, or with up to four climbers sharing one screen, any mix of people and CPU. Pass the device when the panel tells you to.
  • Climbers race the same crag. Who leads is random each route. Climbers later in the order may start with extra drops above their ceiling, tuned per route, because turn order is worth different amounts on a short boulder problem and on a big wall.
  • That head start sits ABOVE your ceiling. The mat has 8 drop counters, but only the first 5 can ever be recovered, marked by the red line. Spend the extras and they are gone, and a pump-out refills you to 5 like everyone else.
  • With four on the wall you will get blocked. If nothing above you is free, resting recovers 2 drops instead of 1, so waiting out the traffic builds you a bigger tank.
  • When a climber tops out, everyone else gets one last turn to match it. If more than one tops out, most water left wins, then most first ascents.
